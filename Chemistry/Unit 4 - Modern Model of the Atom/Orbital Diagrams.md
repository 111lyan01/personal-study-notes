>[!WARNING] NOTE:
>This requires knowledge of [[Electron Configuration]]

The `S`, `P`, `D`, and `F` groups have 1, 3, 5, and 7 orbitals respectively. Remember that each orbital can hold 2 electrons. To draw orbital diagrams, you arrange every part of the element's electron configuration (excluding the <sup>superscript</sup> parts) and draw arrows to indicate whether or not 1 or 2 electrons have filled the orbital. For example:
>[!NOTE] Sodium
>**Electron Configuration:** $1s^2 \space 2s^2 \space 2p^6 \space 3s^1$
>**Orbital Diagram:** $$\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{1s}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{2s}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{2p}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{2p}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{2p}\frac{\uparrow}{3s}$$

Because there are 3 orbitals in the `P` group, there are 3 slots for $2p$ alone. Sodium also has 11 electrons (from its atomic number). Another example:
>[!NOTE] Zinc
>**Electron Configuration:** $1s^2 \space 2s^2 \space 2p^6 \space 3s^2 \space 3p^6 \space 4s^2 \space 3d^{10}$
>**Orbital Diagram:** $$\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{1s}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{2s}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{2p}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{2p}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{2p}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{3s}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{3p}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{3p}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{3p}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{4s}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{3d}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{3d}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{3d}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{3d}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{3d}$$

Here's one where you have to be careful, because there can be no blank spaces left, and if you end up having blank sections left, they must be filled by at least 1 electron:
>[!NOTE] Iron
>**Electron Configuration:** $1s^2 \space 2s^2 \space 2p^6 \space 3s^2 \space 3p^6 \space 4s^2 \space 3d^6$
>**Orbital Diagram:** $$\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{1s}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{2s}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{2p}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{2p}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{2p}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{3s}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{3p}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{3p}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{3p}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{4s}\frac{\uparrow\downarrow}{3d}\frac{\uparrow}{3d}\frac{\uparrow}{3d}\frac{\uparrow}{3d}\frac{\uparrow}{3d}$$
