Lets try to find the electron configuration of oxygen. To find out the electron configuration, we need to locate oxygen on the periodic table. 
* On the periodic table, there are sections highlighted as `S`, `D`, `P`, and `F`. 
	* The `S` block covers the first two columns
	* the `D` block covers the 3rd to 12th columns
	* the `P` block covers the 13th to 18th columns
	* the `F` block covers the lanthanides and actinides
		* except **Lu** and **Lr** which belong in the `D` block
* On the left side of the table, there is a table labeled `EC↓` 
	* below it is `SDP`
	* It labels, in columns, the numbers in the `S`, `D`,  `P`, and `F` blocks

So, if you were to try and find the `5s` row, you would see the numbers below `S` on the table, and go down until you found `5`. The elements in the `5s` row are only the elements within the S-block, so in this case, the elements `Rb` and `Sr` are in the `5s` row.

The electron configuration of Phosporus is $1s^2 \space 2s^2 \space 2p^6 \space 3s^2 \space 3p^3$. How is this the electron configuration? We already know that the $1s$ and $2s$ and so on horizontal sections, but the $^2$ and $^6$ and $^3$ are specifically how many steps (to the right) in the horizontal sections the element reaches. Phosphorus goes through 2 full rows and 5 steps into the 3rd row. The first two rows cover all of the `1s`, `2s`, and `2p` groups. `S` groups max out at 2 steps to the right, and `P` groups max out at 6 steps to the right. So therefore, these three groups are $1s^2 \space 2s^2 \space 2p^6$. On the last row, Phosporus goes through the entire `3s` group (2 steps), and 3 setps into the `P` group, thus filling the rest of the numbers: $3s^2 \space 3p^3$
