### The Dilemma of the atom
**The Dilemma of the atom is that** Electrons outside of the nucleus are attracted to the protons inside the nucleus
**JJ Thomson discovered that electrons** are like particles
**George Thomson discovered that electrons** are like energy waves
**Louis deBroglie discovered that electrons** are both like particles and energy waves, that move through space as a wave.
**Electromagnetic radiation moves through space** as waves at $3 \times 10^8 m/s$.
**$c=f\lambda$ represents** that the speed of light = frequency over wavelength
**$E = hf$ represents that** energy is proportional to the frequency of radiation
**Wavelength affects energy and frequency:**  $$\searrow \lambda = \space \nearrow E \nearrow F \\ \nearrow \lambda = \searrow E \searrow f$$ 
**Frequency affects wavelength and energy:** $$\nearrow f = \searrow \lambda \nearrow E \\ \searrow f = \nearrow \lambda \searrow E$$
**The answer to the dilemma of the atom is** that electrons are waves. As electrons get closer, $\lambda$ is shorter. $\searrow \lambda = \nearrow E$, but $\nearrow E = \nearrow distance \space from \space atom$

**Niels Bohr believed** that electrons orbit the nucleus like planets orbiting the sun (wrong)
**The Heisenbuerg Uncertainty principle states that**

> One cannot simultaneously determine both the position and momentum of an electron

So you can't find where an electron is and where it is going at the same time.
