## Finding Average Atomic Mass
To find the average atomic mass, multiply the mass number and abundance percentage converted to a decimal. Makes groups of these in accordance to how many isotopes are given.

>[!NOTE] Example
>Find the average mass of these isotopes if <sup>85</sup>Rb has an abundance of 72.2% and <sup>87</sup>Rb has an abundance of 27.8%
>$$(85 \times 0.722) + (87 \times 0.278) = 85.556g/mol$$
>
>Magnesium consists of three naturally occurring isotopes. The percent abundance of these follows: <sup>24</sup>Mg (78.70%), <sup>25</sup>Mg (10.13%), <sup>26</sup>Mg (11.7%). The average atomic mass of the three is 24.3050 amu. If the atomic mass of  <sup>25</sup>Mg 24.98584 amu, and <sup>26</sup>Mg is 25.98259 amu, calculate the actual atomic mass of <sup>24</sup>Mg.
>$$(x*0.7870) + (0.1013*24.98584) + (0.117 * 25.98259) = 24.3050$$
>$0.7870x + 2.531065592 + 3.03996303 = 24.3050$
>$0.7870x + 5.571028622 = 24.3050$
>$0.7870x = 18.73397138$
>$x = 23.80428383$

**The molar mass (reported in the periodic table) is the average of all isotopes of that element**.
