Density is how packed a substance is. It is the mass of an object per unit volume it occupies. The common unit expressions of density are $g/cm^2$ and $g/mL$. These expressions are equivalent to each other, so something that is $5g/cm^2$ is also $5g/mL$. Water is the baseline for density, being $1g/cm^3$ and $1g/mL$.

To determine the density of a substance: $$density=\frac{mass}{volume}$$
You can also find the volume of a substance: $$volume=\frac{mass}{density}$$
And finally, you can find the mass: $$mass=density \times volume$$
>[!NOTE] Examples
>An object has a mass of $8.53g$ and a volume of $4.90cm^3$:
>$$density=\frac{8.53g}{4.90cm^3} = 1.74g/cm^3$$
>An object has a density of $8.96g/cm^3$ and a mass of $25g$:
>$$volume = \frac{25g}{8.96g/cm^3}=2.79cm^3$$
>An object has a density of $11.34g/cm^3$ and a volume of $2 L$:
>$$2 L = 2000mL$$
>$$mass = 11.34g/cm^3 \times 2000mL = 23,000g/mL$$
