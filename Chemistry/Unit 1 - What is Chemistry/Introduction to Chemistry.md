## Terms
### Science
Experimental Investigations and explainations of naturally occuring events
### Technology
The application of scientific technology
### Chemistry
The study of matter and its transformations
### Matter
Anything with mass and form
### Transformation
Changes in matter
### Pure Substance
A single type of matter
### Mixture
Two or more subtances intermingled
## Mixtures, Substances, and Elements
### Homogenous Mixture
Mixtures with a uniform composition
### Heterogenous Mixture
Mixtures with a varied composition with mixed ratios across substances

>[!NOTE] Examples
>Piece of wood: **Heterogenous mixture**
>Iron nail: **Pure substance**
>Rusty iron nail: **Heterogenous mixture**
>Well-stirred mixture of food dye and water: **Homogenous mixture**
>Beeswax and candle wax hand-mixed: **Heterogenous mixture**
>Beeswax and candle wax melted, stirred, and allowed to solidify: **Homogenous mixture**
### Elements
The building blocks of all matter, 118 (as of now) discovered on the periodic table
### Elemental Symbols
Represented with one or two letters.
### Atoms
The smallest structural units of elements
### Pure Substances Types
**Elements:** Only one element
**Compounds:** 2+ elements
### Compounds
A pure substance of 2+ elements (also a molecule)

>[!NOTE] Examples
>Fe<sub>2</sub>O<sub>3</sub> is a **compound**, and can also be considered a **molecule**
>O<sub>3</sub> is **a molecule and not a compound**
>Fe is **an element**
>CO is a **compound and molecule**
>C<sub>3</sub>H<sub>8</sub> is a **compound and molecule**

>[!WARNING] Notice
>Molecules that are **not compounds** are simply elements that have more than one of itself, for example: O<sub>2</sub>, Na<sub>3</sub>, Cu<sub>5</sub>
## Physical and Chemical Change
### Chemical Formula
How much of each atom is needed to make the samllest unit of the compound
### Physical Transformation
The substance in a different state of matter
### States of Matter Changes

| Term         | Phase Change    |
| ------------ | --------------- |
| Freezing     | Liquid to solid |
| Melting      | Solid to liquid |
| Vaporization | Liquid to gas   |
| Condensation | Gas to liquid   |
| Sublimation  | Solid to gas    |
| Deposition   | Gas to solid    |
Water melting point: $0 \celsius$
Water boiling point: $100 \celsius$

>[!NOTE] Example
>Ice cubes slowly vanishing while stored in a freezer is an example of **sublimation**.
### Chemical Transformation
When a substance changes into another substance
### Chemical Reaction
Chemical transformations
### Reactants and Products
Starting substances and products of the reactions
**g**: gas
**l**: liquid
**s**: solid
**aq**: aqueous
## Science
### The Scientific Method
Question > Hypotheosis > Experiment > Analyze > Conclusion
### Hypothesis vs Theory
**Hypothesis**: First time a theory is postulated
**Theory**: Surpported and well-tested hypothesis
### Intensive vs Extensive Property
**Intensive**: Not dependent on the amount of matter present
**Extensive**: Depdendent on the amount of matter present
## Extra: Filtration and Distillation
You use **filtration** to seperate liquids and solids, such as sugar and water, or sand and water. **Distillation** seperates two substances from a mixture, such as alcohol and water.
