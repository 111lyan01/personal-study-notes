## Congressional Elections
* Elections take place in state legislatures
* Representatives are elected every two years
* US Census (Every 10 years) determines number of reps per state
* Senators elected every two years, strictly two to each state
* Washington, D.C. still has no representation
	* Bills have proposed DC representation but none have passed
## Candidates for Congressional Elections
* Members for the house must be $\ge25$, be a citizen for 7 years and live in the state they represent 
* Senators must be $\ge30$, be a citizen for 9 years, and be a resident of the state they represent
* In congressional districts with a minority party, the leader may recruit candidates
### Congressional Campaigns and Elections
* Campaigns nowadays cost up to $10.2M for Senators and $1.5M for the House
* Funding includes direct contributions, political action committees, and state party committees.
* Laws regulate campaigns such as the Election Campaign Act of 1971 and the Bipartisan Campaign Reform Act of 2002
* Candidates for Congress win nominations through a direct primary
	* Party identifiers vote for the candidate to be on the party ticket in the general election
### Presidential Effects
* Candidates for Congress hope that a presidential candidate will have "coattails" that sweep in senators and representatives of the same party
* Although the effects of recent presidents have not shown this "coattail" effect.
## The Power of Incumbency
* When members are elected and survive the second election, they build loyalty among voters increasing reelection chances
* Developing their homestyle, using media, and making personal appearances build loyalty to produce a favorable image of themselves and maintain party control.
# Congressional Apportionment
* Reapportionment is the allocation of seats in the house to each state
* Redistricting is the redrawing of the boundaries of the congressional districts within states
* The Supreme Court made the apportionment of state legislative districts a justifiable question. They invoked the 14th amendment to say that no state can deny any person the equal protect of the laws
* The court held that both chambers of a state legislature must be apportioned so that all districts are equal in protection
* Later, the court applied "one person, one vote" to congressional districts
## Gerrymandering
* The drawing of legislative district boundary lines in effort to gain a factional advantage
* This can be seen recently with redistricting efforts led by <sub>a particular us president</sub> parties to gain advantages in the midterms.
