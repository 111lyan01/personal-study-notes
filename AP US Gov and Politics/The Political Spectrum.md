## Political Ideologies
A closely linked set of beliefs about politics. The core of a political ideology is a set of philosophies and values that guide the ideology. The most commonly discussed in American politics are liberalism and conservatism.
## The Political Spectrum
The political spectrum places ideologies on a spectrum showing how left or right they are. The left and right may have some different ideologies, but the values can be somewhat grouped together.
### The left
**Socialism** typically advocate for replacing private business ownership with government or ownership by employees. They believe that breakup of the wealthy is necessary and that wealth should be distributed among the people.
### The right
On the right side the **liberatarianism**, whom support property rights and oppose regulation of the economy.
## In the Middle: Liberalism and Conservatism
Beliefs in **liberalism** include government action to support the welfare of the people, support civil rights, and tolerate/accept social change. Liberals support efforts against poverty, wealth redistribution, economy regulation, and are generally positive towards the rights of LGBTQ+. **Conversatives** on the other hand, support less government role in supporting public welfare, believe that the private sector outpreforms the government. They oppose redistribution of wealth and say that people are responsible for their own outcomes.
## Nuance in Liberalism and Conservatism
### Liberalism
The meaning and identity of "liberalism" has changed a lot throughout the years. It first meant "free" simply, and referred to a philosophy of libertarianism. But in the 1800s, liberalism meant opposition to government control. Then in the 1930s liberalism and democracy changed to promote more government intervention. However, outside of the US, liberalism still generally leans to less government intervention and *laissez-faire* capitalism.
### Conservatism
Similarly, conservatism believe in opposing change and maintaining traditions. Yet nowadays there are two types of conservatism: social and economic conservatives. Social conservatives believe in opposing some civil liberties such as LGBTQ+ rights and abortion rights, and support the death penalty and right to own firearms. Economic conservatives believe less in civil rights and more towards less government, and supporting private property and capitalism.
### Libertarianism
Libertarianism follows the beliefs of near absolute freedom. The the case of the economy, they lean right with support for less government intervention, yet on the case of social matters, they advocate for complete freedom in civil rights. They discourage what conservatives support in social and civil rights, and are favored by political liberals in this regard.
