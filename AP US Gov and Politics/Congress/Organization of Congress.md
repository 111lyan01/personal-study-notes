Congress consists of many **committees** that have the final say on pieces of legislation. Legislators defer to the expertise that the chairperson and members of a committee have to defend a committee decision. Committee **chairpersons** exercise control over the hearings and formal action on bills and decide which subcommittees will act on legislation.

A **discharge petition** can be used to deprive a committee control of a bill, if a bill has been considered for more than 30 days.
## Types of Congressional Committees
### Standing Committees
Committees that are permanent and continue from session to session. Most standing committees have subcommittees that carry out their work. Each standing committee is given a specific area of legislative policy jurisdiction. Some committees are considered more important than others, representatives seek to be in committees that control spending and benefit their constituents.
### Select Committees
Temporary committees created for a specific legislative purpose. Select committees rarely create original legislation.
### Joint Committees
Two committees on both chambers of Congress, that may be temporary or permanent.
### Conference Committees
Special join committees that form to achieve parity on the wording of legislative acts when the two chambers pass legislative proposals on different forms. Because bills must pass both the Senate and the House of Representatives, conference committees ensure that the bill is in identical form.
### The House Rules Committee
A special committee that rules the set time limit on debate and determines whether a bill can be amended. This Rules Committee is also able to meet while the House is in session, have its own resolutions considered immediately on the floor, and to initiate legislation on its own.
## Selection of Committee Members
Members are appointed to standing committees by the Steering Committee of their party. The majority-party member with the longest term of service on the committee can have preferences on new chairpersons. The **senority system** designates the older representatives in Congress more position of power within Congress. Members of the House or Senate that were reelected often would accumulate years of committee service to become chairpersons of their committees.
