# House of Representatives Leadership
## The Speaker of the House
The Speaker of the House is the most powerful position in the House of Representatives. The speaker of the house is generally able to:
1. Preside over meetings of the House
2. Appoint members of join committees and conference committees
3. Schedule legislation for floor action
4. Decide points of order and interpret the rules with the advice of the house
5. Referring bills and resolutions to the appropriate standing committees of the House
The speaker of the house is also the person who decides tiebreakers, but can also participate in normal debate and vote like any other representative.
## The Majority Leader
Elected by a caucus of the majority party to increase cohesion among party members and act as a spokesperson. Majority leaders also cooperate with the Speaker and other party leader to form the party's legislative program and guide it through the legislative process in the house.
## The Minority Leader
The minority leader of the house is the candidate nominated for speaker by a caucus of the minority party. The minority leader also consults with the speaker and majority leader on recognizing members who want to speak, house rules and procedure, and scheduling legislation, but they have no actual power in any of these.
## Whips
Whips are members what assist the party leaders by passing information down from the leadership to party members and making sure members appear for floor debate and cast votes. They conduct polls, inform leaders on doubtful votes, and pressure members to support leaders' positions.
