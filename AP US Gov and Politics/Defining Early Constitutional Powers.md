## McCulloch v. Maryland
When the US decided to charter its own national bank in Maryland, the government of Maryland did not want a competing national bank and attempted to put it out of business. The state taxed the bank heavily but the bank's cashier, James William McCulloch, refused to pay. Maryland took McCulloh to state court where Maryland won. The national government appealed to the Supreme Court
### The Constitutional Questions
Congress has the authority to make *all* laws that are deemed necessary and proper. But is chartering a national bank necessary and proper? Does the clause mean *necessary* in a strict way where the creation of a national bank is not necessary, or does *necessary* not conform to such a strict model? Even so, if the national bank is constitutional, can the state of Maryland tax it?
### Decision
John Marshall gave the decision. According to Marshall, the national bank was created in order to aid the government in its operations, then it falls under the elastic clause. Therefore, Marshall gave the elastic clause a looser interpretation allowing the Constitution to stay flexible over time.
## Gibbons v. Odgen
In Article I, Section 8, the **commerce clause** is defined. It says that Congress has the ability to regulate commerce among the several states. There were questions raised, however on what "commerce" meant and what it was to be regulated among the states?
### Background
Aaron Ogden operated ferryboats between New York and New Jersey and was permitted by the New York state. Thomas Gibbons also got a license to operating in interstate waters but from the US government and did not ask for the permission of New York. Ogden sued Gibbons and the state of New York prevented Gibbons from operating further. Gibbons appealed to the Supreme Court. There were several questions raised on this subject. What is included in the word "commerce"? Does Congress's ability to regulate commerce into intrastate commerce? Is the ability to regular commerce interstate a state thing or a federal thing or both?
### Decision
Marshall defined commerce as all commercial interaction and all business dealings, which includes steamboat navigation and people transport. States have the ability to regular commerce even if it could not reach solely intrastate commerce, but the power to regular interstate commerce is exclusively a national power. So Gibbons won in this case, because he was authorized by the federal government and the state can't prohibit his federal license
## States vs The Federal Government
When John Marshall was chief justice of the Supreme Court, he mainly increased the power of the federal government, but during 1829-1837 (The Jacksonian Era), states began to regain power over the federal government. States tried to ultimately give themselves the right to pull out of the Union and establish their own government, but the Civil War ended this idea of states leaving the US.
## The Growth of National Government
The Civil War led to increased national power as a result. Union war efforts led to new things that increased federal power, such as income tax, restricted civil liberty for war, and greater social roles for the nation. Throughout the Civil War, the federal government increased its authority. **Three Amendments were passed** as a result of the Civil War, the thirteenth, which made slavery illegal, the fourteenth, which guaranteed equal rights under state law for all citizens, and the fifteenth, which gave African Americans the right to vote in elections.

## STILL: States vs The Federal Government
Following the Civil War, a new political model was followed called **Dual Federalism**, in which it depicts the national and state government as separate governing bodies that should not intervene with each other.
### The Supreme Court
The Supreme Court had its power reduced in the period following the Civil War due to its opinion on the various conflicts of slavery. Congress exempted some of the Supreme Courts decisions and powers of judicial review as a result of the Civil War. Once the Supreme Court began to actually be taken seriously again, it leaned in favor of states rights and dual federalism.

## The New Deal and Cooperative Federalism
Once the policies like the New Deal began, the government reversed directions once again and broke Dual Federalism in favor of Cooperative Federalism, in which the states and federal government cooperate to solve problems. The government began heavy involvement in social, civil, and economic change once again. 
### Categorical vs Block Grants
Categorical grants are funds to help state programs using formulas based on certain variables. These funds are specific to certain issues and offer a way for the government to influence state conflicts. Block grants are able to fund state governments and group several categorical grants under one broad heading.
