## Powers of the National Government
Enumerated powers are powers specifically written down in the Consitution. But there are also **implied powers** that do not need to be explicitly written down. The **necessary and proper** clause specifies that **all** laws that are necessary and proper for the country Congress has the ability to make.
>[!NOTE] The Constitution
>*[Congress shall have the ability to] make all Laws which shall be necessary and proper for carrying into Execution the foregoing Powers, and all other Powers vesting by this Constitution in the Government of the United States, or in any Department or Officer thereof*

Inherent powers are national powers that are not implied in the necessary and proper clause. They are necessary because the federal government can be the only government that deals with situations from other countries
## State Powers
10th Amendment says whatever's not illegal to the states or given to the US government, are given to the states or people. There are also reserved powers for the states that the federal government **cannot deny**. States also have **police power**, which is the authority to legislate for the protection of the people. They can pass laws on marrriage, crimes, education, intrastate transportation, etc.
