The US is a mix of authority from states, the nation, and local cities. Oftentimes, authority for the government is opposed from states who want to keep their authority.

>[!INFO] Three Systems of Government
>**Unitary System**
>Ultimate governmental authority to the central government
>**Confederal System**
>A league of independent states with a loose central government only capable of handling issues states bring up to it.
>**Federal System**
>Divided authority across central and regional governments.

>[!NOTE] Why Federalism?
>The distribution of power in a Federal government is a very important question, as too much power in one once is a risk to liberty. Federalism provides a way to ensure stability while also giving states power to the areas closest to everyday citizens' lives.
# A Practical Constitutional Solution
People wanting a strong central government clashed with those wanting more state rights, resulting in the many compromises of the Constitution. People realized that a unitary system of government, even if unopposed, would not work at the scales of the colonies. Federalism does not have this problem since each level can work more independently and closer to the people
## Benefits for the United States
Federalism allowed for many things. State governments could be used as training groups for future national leaders and policies to prove themselves and become national.
## Allowance for Many Political Subcultures
America has many subcultures divided on  the lines of race, ethnic origin, region, wealth, education, etc. The diversity of these subcultures were seen a challenge where Madison argued that a Federal government would protect people from the power of unjust majority of national government.

The strength of Federalism in allowing political subcultures means that a greater variety of interests can influence government and drive reform.
## Arguments Against Federalism
Some argued that Federalism is a way to block progress with state and local interests. Minority groups can be blocked out by majority groups. Another concern is if Americans would suffer from the inequalities that exist across the several states. These people generally argued for greater federal legislation. Others saw opposite. There would be a danger of national powers at the expense of the smaller states.
